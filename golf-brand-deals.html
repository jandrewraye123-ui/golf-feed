How golf influencer sponsorships and brand deals work

Golf creators are not just entertainers anymore. They are brands, and the companies that partner with them are betting big on their audiences. Here is how creator brand deals work in golf.

The main types of creator brand deals

Sponsored videos

The simplest deal is a brand paying to be featured in a video, either as a quick mention or a full segment built around the product.

Apparel and merch lines

The biggest creator groups sell their own clothing. Good Good built its apparel line into a real business, with retail partners like Dick’s Sporting Goods carrying its products before this year’s controversy.

Co-branded products

Some creators go further and put their name on equipment made with a major brand. Good Good and Callaway released a co-branded line that included two drivers and golf balls.

Events and title sponsorships

In October 2025, the PGA Tour announced the Good Good Championship in Austin, the first time a digital-first golf brand was set to be a title sponsor of a Tour event.

Investment

Investors are paying attention too. Good Good raised $45 million in 2025 in a round led by Creator Sports Capital, with more than 50 investors including Peyton Manning.

Brands acting like creators

Golf companies now run their own channels. TaylorMade has more than a million YouTube subscribers, which shows how much brands want the same attention creators get.

Case study: Good Good, Callaway, and the 2026 driver ad

In late August 2026, Good Good released an ad for its co-branded Callaway driver. In it, co-founder Garrett Clark shoves fellow creator Alexis Miestowski to the ground when she reaches for his driver. Good Good said it was meant as a parody of the movie Obsession, but the ad drew immediate criticism for depicting violence against a woman.

Both companies deleted the video and apologized. Callaway CEO Chip Brewer said the ad was approved by Callaway before it was posted and that the approval should never have happened. The fallout spread quickly. Callaway ended the partnership, retailers including Dick’s Sporting Goods, Golf Galaxy, and PGA Tour Superstore pulled the merchandise, and Good Good lost its title sponsorships of Golf Channel’s Big Break revival and the November PGA Tour event. CEO Matt Kendrick and president Joe Flannery left the company, and co-founder Nahid Giga stepped in as interim CEO.

The audience reaction was more mixed. Good Good told Front Office Sports that its online store had its biggest traffic days ever in the last week of August, and that about two thirds of those buyers were first-time customers.

What this means for creator marketing

Once creators sign deals with major brands and sponsor pro events, they get held to the same brand safety standards as traditional sponsors. Loyal fans can stick with a creator through a mistake, but partners and retailers may not. For brands, it is a reminder that approving creator content needs the same review process as any other ad.
